How much AI should I use?

A guide for environmental majors. Pick the kind of assessment and how well you know the skill. You’ll get a recommendation for each part of the task, not just the assignment as a whole.

The rule of thumb: use AI for what you’ve already learned to do, not for what you’re there to learn.

!

Check your course policy first

Your instructor’s AI policy always comes first. This guide helps you decide how much AI to use within what your course allows. If the syllabus says no AI, the answer is no AI. Not sure what’s allowed? Ask your instructor before you start. If you do use AI, follow your course’s rules for acknowledging it. If AI is part of a disability accommodation, your accommodation plan comes first.

Four levels of AI use

From most to least of your own thinking.

LEVEL 0

No AI

Your brain only. This is the learning itself.

LEVEL 1

AI Coach

AI adds productive struggle: hints, questions, quizzes, critique. It never hands you the answer. A TA, librarian or classmate can coach you too.

LEVEL 2

AI Assist

AI removes extraneous load: plain-language explanations, debugging help, reformatting. You still do the thinking.

LEVEL 3

AI Offload

AI does the step and you check it. Only for steps that aren’t the learning goal, and only if you could spot an error.

Check your task

Five quick questions.

1What kind of assessment is it?
2When did you last do this kind of work without AI and check it was right?

Not “how confident do you feel?” Confidence is exactly what AI inflates. Answer with what you’ve actually done.

3Which of these are learning goals in your syllabus? (tick any)

If the course is teaching a skill, you don’t hand that skill to AI. Not sure? Check the syllabus learning outcomes.

4If AI got something wrong in this task, would you notice?
5How long have you already worked on this on your own?
